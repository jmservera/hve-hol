Exercise 1 · Open lab 1 · Whole-room warm-up · No agents

Predict the lifecycle before the agents do

Core taskOpen lab 1

On this pageBriefUse caseHand-in and toolkitSix roundsTips
Objective

Understand each HVE step: what it needs, what it produces, and where a person decides.

Input
The same fictional citizen-appointment change request for every team
Best use
Facilitator-led thinking exercise; no agents
Roles
Whole team; auditor writes the answers; facilitator leads the room

Steps

  1. The facilitator presents the fictional use case below.

  2. For each of six HVE steps, listen to one guided question.

  3. Write your team’s short answer in 90 seconds, on paper or in the web answer sheet.

  4. Only then open that round’s reveal with the facilitator: what is produced, and who decides?

  5. Score your original answer: ✓ we had it · ½ partly · ✗ we missed it.

Hand in

Tick items as your team hands them in.

How it works

About 30 minutes: a short introduction, six answer-and-reveal rounds, then a recap. Use the web sheet or paper, but do not ask an agent. Keep your original answers after the reveals; a defensible different answer is a different judgement, not automatically a miss.

The shared use case

Cita Previa: change an appointment

Six guided rounds

Answer first, then reveal one step at a time

Round 1 · Evidence

Before anyone writes code, what should the agent read, and what should it hand back to you?

Write your answer in the answer sheet or on paper. Take 90 seconds; wait for the facilitator before opening the reveal.

Reveal · Read before you writeOpen after answering, then score your original answer.

Illustrative example · fictional client and system · not a recorded run

  • Evidence summary: fewer phone calls (CITA-482); 15-minute slots, 30-day advance booking, email and SMS confirmation (Confluence).
  • Five open questions: how many changes per citizen; how many hours before; another office too; who is notified; same-day appointments?

Human decision: The business owner confirms sources and completeness. Nothing is approved yet.

Round 2 · Requirements

The agent drafts the requirements. Name 3 things it must ASK the business owner instead of deciding itself.

Write your answer in the answer sheet or on paper. Take 90 seconds; wait for the facilitator before opening the reveal.

Reveal · The agent asks; the owner decidesOpen after answering, then score your original answer.

Illustrative example · fictional client and system · not a recorded run

  • Requirements v0.2: R3 permits at most 2 changes (owner answer 1); R4 closes changes 24 hours before (owner answer 2); R5 frees the old slot immediately (Confluence).
  • AC-7: under 24 hours, Change date is disabled and the office phone is shown. Accessibility requirement: WCAG 2.1 AA for the public-sector example.
  • Unanswered rules and their dependent acceptance criteria remain blocked.

Human decision: The business owner answers an options/undecided form and approves the requirements.

Round 3 · Research

Before planning, what 3 facts must the agent find out in the code?

Write your answer in the answer sheet or on paper. Take 90 seconds; wait for the facilitator before opening the reveal.

Reveal · Check the code before trusting the docsOpen after answering, then score your original answer.

Illustrative example · fictional client and system · not a recorded run

  • Booking logic is in AppointmentService; cancellation already releases a slot.
  • A shared messaging service exists, but new SMS text is needed. There are no automated AppointmentService tests.
  • Confluence says 30 days; the code allows 60. That conflict becomes a new business-owner question, not an agent-made decision.

Human decision: No approval yet. The evidence and the documentation/code conflict go back to the owner.

Round 4 · Plan

The agent proposes a plan. What would make you REJECT it?

Write your answer in the answer sheet or on paper. Take 90 seconds; wait for the facilitator before opening the reveal.

Reveal · Reject the plan before any code existsOpen after answering, then score your original answer.

Illustrative example · fictional client and system · not a recorded run

  • Plan: 1 safety-net tests for current booking; 2 R3/R4 rules; 3 reuse slot release R5; 4 SMS/email text; 5 disable the button under 24 hours (AC-7); 6 accessibility checks.
  • Critique: a displayed slot may be taken before confirmation. Add task 2b to hold the slot at confirmation.

Human decision: The tech lead or architect approves the plan before any code is written.

Round 5 · Implement

How much freedom would you give the agent here, and what proves the work is done?

Write your answer in the answer sheet or on paper. Take 90 seconds; wait for the facilitator before opening the reveal.

Reveal · Match autonomy to riskOpen after answering, then score your original answer.

Illustrative example · fictional client and system · not a recorded run

  • Tasks 1–6 and 2b are done; new AppointmentService tests pass; changes link back to R3–R5 and AC-7.
  • Because the service lacked tests, the engineering lead chooses a check-in after each task. Manual is the default; full autonomy is only on request.

Human decision: The engineering lead chooses the autonomy level and checks progress against evidence.

Round 6 · Review and hand-off

What can the agent review on its own, and what must a person still check before release?

Write your answer in the answer sheet or on paper. Take 90 seconds; wait for the facilitator before opening the reveal.

Reveal · Finished is not acceptedOpen after answering, then score your original answer.

Illustrative example · fictional client and system · not a recorded run

  • Acceptance tests are verified except for the manual screen-reader announcement of the new date. SMS text over 160 characters was found and fixed.
  • The owner still has to try the test environment. A backlog item to show remaining changes is proposed, not created.

Human decision: The business owner accepts; a named person approves the GitLab merge request; the normal release process still applies.

Six steps, five human decisions: sources confirmed · requirements approved · plan approved · autonomy chosen · merge approved.

Tips

  • Wait for the facilitator before opening each reveal. These are fictional illustrations, not measurements or a recorded run.
  • Keep your answer sheet for Exercise 10 (backup): compare the human decisions you predicted with the effort your real run needed.
  • The inventory-aware cart is a separate fallback scenario, not this exercise’s appointment case.
